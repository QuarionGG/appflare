Appflare now watches what each installed app uses, every five minutes. It reads Cloudflare's analytics for the app's own resources (its Workers, Durable Objects, D1 databases, KV namespaces, R2 buckets, queues, Vectorize indexes, Analytics Engine datasets and container applications; Workers AI is shared evenly by the apps that bind it) and keeps its own hourly history, which outlives a deleted Durable Object namespace. Then it acts on spend protection: it tells you when an app's usage is far above its normal (once per episode), when a usage alert's sentence comes true (once per period), and on the Free plan when a free daily limit is about to run out today; it pauses an app that loops (two checks in a row) or passes its spending cap when automatic pausing is on; and with a monthly budget it pauses the app with the largest share of the month's overage, one per check, once this month's Appflare usage, including the $5 plan, is on pace to pass it. On Workers Free, whose invocations get 10 ms of CPU time each, a check reads the analytics in one request and the database in a few batches, and splits its work across invocations: the check reads and stores the usage, a second one works out the account's numbers, and parts of four apps each judge their apps. Apps installed in the last 15 minutes are read from their next check on, since Cloudflare's analytics show a new app only after about that long. When the Cloudflare connection may not read analytics, nothing is read and the pages can say to reconnect.

The scheduled run now happens every five minutes instead of every 30 (still one cron trigger, of the five the Free plan allows per account); everything it did before still happens every 30 minutes. Cron triggers belong to the Worker rather than to a version, so each version of Appflare now sets its own schedule: its first scheduled run after an update replaces the Worker's schedule when it differs (and tries again on the next run if Cloudflare refuses). Rolling Appflare back sets the schedule the earlier version ran with, every 30 minutes for versions from before this one.

Usage now includes Workflow steps ($0.80 per 100,000 past the 500,000 a month Workers Paid includes; 3,000 a day on Free). A Workflow's CPU time and runs count as its Worker's, as Cloudflare bills them.
