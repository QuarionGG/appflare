A Worker that loops can run up a bill overnight, or use up a free daily limit that
every app in the account shares. Appflare reads what each installed app uses every
5 minutes, shows it on Home and on the app's page, tells you when something looks
wrong, and can pause an app that runs away. Pausing is off until you turn it on.

Admins change everything on this page. Members see the same numbers and settings,
without the buttons.

## What Appflare watches

Every 5 minutes, Appflare reads Cloudflare's analytics for the resources it created for
each app: its Workers, Durable Objects, D1 databases, KV namespaces, R2 buckets,
queues, Workflows, Vectorize indexes, Analytics Engine datasets, container
applications, and the Browser Run sessions of its Workers. It matches usage to apps by the resources' ids, not by name, and it keeps
its own hourly history: 45 days of hours and 400 days of daily totals. That history
outlives a deleted Durable Object namespace, whose analytics Cloudflare deletes with it.

Cloudflare's analytics are a few minutes behind, and they can still change for a
while. Each check reads the last 3 hours again.

**A new app shows up after about an hour.** Appflare starts reading an app 15 minutes
after it is installed, from the moment it was installed, but Cloudflare's analytics
name a new Worker only after 40 to 50 minutes. Until its usage appears, for up to an
hour, the app counts as just installed: its card on Home and its **Usage** tab say
**Usage shows up about an hour after install**, and alerts and automatic pausing cannot
see it yet. To watch a new app's requests sooner, its
**Usage** tab offers **Turn on Workers Logs**: Workers Logs in the Cloudflare dashboard show new requests
within a couple of minutes. Updates and settings changes keep the app's Workers Logs
as they are.

All money is an estimate: usage times Cloudflare's list price. See
[What the numbers are](#what-the-numbers-are).

## Where you see it

### Home

**Usage** sits above **Your apps**. It covers the whole Cloudflare account, by app.

- On Workers Paid, three totals: **Usage this month** (what the account used past what
  the $5 plan includes), **Projected month** (the bill with the $5 plan, if the last
  day's pace holds) and **Burning now** (what every app's activity costs an hour at list
  price, before included usage). Paused apps count as using nothing more, and removed
  apps count only for what they used before.
- On Workers Free: **Can be billed** ($0, unless R2 is past its free amount, see
  [below](#workers-free-and-workers-paid)), **Closest daily limit** (the free daily
  limit the account has used most of today) and **Apps needing a look**.
- A chart of the last 7 days, hour by hour, for the product you pick in the tabs above
  it, stacked by app with one color per app. Past six apps, the smallest share one
  color as **Other apps**.

Each app's card adds a small 7-day chart of its most telling product and one line on
its usage, such as "12% of today's requests" or "Paused · no usage since". The chart
turns red when the app runs far above its normal or used up a free daily limit, and
grey while it is paused. In those cases the card also leads with **Pause** or
**Resume** for admins.

### Needs attention

Spend protection puts its rows at the top of **Needs attention** on Home:

- an app **using far more than usual**, or with no usual usage yet and **already using
  a lot** ([unusual usage](#unusual-usage)), with **Pause app**;
- a free daily limit **used up** for today, with **Pause app**;
- an app that is **paused**, with **Resume**;
- a free daily limit **about to run out** today, with **See usage**.

The sidebar and the app's card show a dot for each.

![Needs attention with Service status using far more than usual: Durable Objects requests at 4.4M an hour, 3,000× its normal, about $0.66 an hour, with See usage and Pause app](/screenshots/usage-needs-attention.png)

### The app's Usage tab

Every installed app's page has a **Usage** tab, after **Overview**.

- One line on how the app is doing: normal, busier than usual (an hour at 3 times its
  normal or more, which alerts no one), far above normal with the numbers, a free
  daily limit used up, paused and why, or just installed. A banner above the tab
  offers **Pause app** or **Resume** when it applies.
- A chart of the product you pick over 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, with a line at the
  app's normal hour and markers for updates, settings changes, alerts and pauses.
- A table of every product the app uses: the last 24 hours, today, this month, where
  the month is heading (**Projected**), how much of what the plan includes the account
  has used (or of the free limit, on Workers Free), and on Workers Paid the app's part
  of the **Cost past included**. Select a row to chart it.
- **Spend protection**: the app's own [alerts](#alerts) and
  [automatic pausing](#pause-apps-automatically), and for admins **Pause app** while
  the app is running.
- **Recent activity**: updates, settings changes, alerts, pauses and resumes, newest
  first.

## Alerts

Each alert goes to the [notification channels](/guides/notifications/) that want its
event, and the app's **Recent activity** records it. Unusual usage and free daily
limits also show on Home under **Needs attention**. A message about an app links to its
**Usage** tab, and every dollar amount in one is an estimate.

Appflare sends each alert once: once per episode of unusual usage, once per usage
alert and period, once per app and day for a free daily limit. An alert that keeps
holding does not repeat on every check.

### Unusual usage

An app's **normal** is its usual busy hour: the 95th percentile of the hours it was
active over the 7 days before the last day. The last day is left out, so a loop that
started yesterday does not become the normal it is measured against. Idle hours do
not count, so an app that runs a batch once a day is compared with its batches. An app
with fewer than 3 active hours in that window has no normal yet.

An hour is unusual when one of the app's products reaches the set multiple of its
normal for that product. Choose it on the app's **Usage** tab under **Unusual usage**:
**At 5× its normal**, **At 20× its normal** (the default), **At 100× its normal**, or
**Don't alert**. **Settings > Your account > Spend protection > Unusual usage alerts**
sets the default for every app.

The hour must also matter, so a tiny app does not alert on noise: on Workers Paid it
must cost at least $0.05 at list price, and on Workers Free it must use at least 5% of
a free daily limit (or cost $0.05 of R2, which bills on Free too).

An app with no normal yet cannot be far above it, but a new app that loops must not
go unnoticed for a week. It raises the same alert once it uses a lot: over $1 an hour
on Workers Paid, or on Workers Free, on pace to use at least 25% of a free daily limit
within the day (or R2 activity over $1 an hour). The message calls the app new only in
its first 7 days; after that it is an app with no usual usage yet, such as one that
runs rarely.

The message (**Unusual usage**, `usage_unusual`) names the product, how far above
normal it is, and what the app costs an hour and has cost today. The episode ends when
no product is even half that far above its normal any more (for an app with no normal,
when it uses less than half that much). The next one sends a new message.

After a resume, the check counts only what the app used since the resume, so the loop
it was paused for does not alert again.

### Usage alerts

A usage alert is a sentence you write on the app's **Usage** tab, under **Usage
alerts** > **Add alert**:

> When it [has spent | is on pace to spend] [amount] [in dollars | of included usage]
> [today | this week | this month]

For example, "When it has spent $6.00 this month" or "When it is on pace to use 80% of
included usage by the end of the week".

- **Has spent** compares what the app has used so far in the period.
- **Is on pace to spend** compares where it ends up by the end of the period at its
  current pace: what it used so far plus the pace times the hours left. The pace is the
  faster of the last 3 hours and the last 24 hours' average, so a quiet stretch does
  not hide a busy day. When the last 3 hours fall below a tenth of the day's average
  (the app stopped, or was paused), only they count.
- **In dollars** is the app's activity at list price, before included usage. Storage
  does not count.
- **Of included usage** is the app's own use as a share of what Workers Paid includes,
  for the product where that share is largest. A daily allowance, such as Workers AI's,
  counts once for each day of the period.
- Periods are UTC: a day starts at 00:00 UTC, a week on Monday at 00:00 UTC, and a
  month on the 1st.

Each alert shows where it stands, with a bar for what is spent and where the pace
leads: **Watching**, **Close** (at 80% of the amount or more), **On pace to pass**, or
**Alert sent**. It sends one message (**Usage alert**, `usage_alert`) the first time
it comes true in a period, and none again until the next period.

On Workers Free, a usage alert is a share of a free daily limit, for today only, such
as "When it is on pace to use 50% of a free daily limit by the end of the day". Dollar
alerts and weeks or months do not apply there and show **Not used**.

### Free daily limit running out

On Workers Free, the daily limits belong to the account, not to an app: 100,000 Worker
requests a day are shared by every app. Once one is used up, Cloudflare refuses more
until 00:00 UTC, so apps that need it fail until then. The limits are in the
[table below](#what-the-numbers-are).

When the account is on pace to use up a daily limit before 00:00 UTC, Appflare tells
you about the app using most of it (**Free daily limit running out**,
`usage_limit_near`). Each app gets at most one such message a day, about the limit
closest to running out; another limit, used most by another app, sends its own. Home shows when it runs out at this pace, then that it is used
up. R2 has no daily limit: it bills past its monthly free amount instead.

## Pause an app

Admins can pause any running app from its **Usage** tab, with **Pause app** in its
**Spend protection** section. When an app is far above normal or has used up a free
daily limit, Home and the tab's banner offer **Pause app** too. Appflare asks first, then starts a pause job with
its own log. Only one job of an app runs at a time.

### What a pause does

Each of the app's Workers serves a stand-in version in place of the app's code, at
100%. The stand-in keeps every binding and secret and runs no Durable Object
migration, so nothing the app stored is touched: its KV, D1, R2, Durable Objects and
queues keep their data.

- Every request gets a 503 page that says **This app is paused by its owner**. Static
  files are not served either.
- Durable Object alarms are put off by an hour each time they fire, so an alarm loop
  stops costing and each object keeps its alarm for later.
- A hibernating WebSocket that sends a message is closed with code 1012, "Paused".
- Cron triggers do nothing, and incoming email is rejected.
- Other Workers that call the app over a service binding get the paused page, or an
  error from its methods.
- The app's queues stop delivering. Messages wait in the queue.
- Running Workflow instances are paused, and a new instance fails at once.
- Container applications are allowed no instances, and running ones stop within about
  15 seconds.

Requests get the paused page within a few seconds. A Durable Object that holds an
ordinary (non-hibernating) WebSocket can go on running the app's own code for a few
minutes, until Cloudflare closes the connection.

The pause records the version each Worker served, and every queue, container
application and Workflow instance it changes, before it changes it. Storage is still
billed while an app is paused.

The app's activity records the pause, and the notification channels get **App
paused** (`app_paused`), with who paused it, or why Appflare did.

### Resume

Admins select **Resume** on Home or on the app's **Usage** tab. The resume job puts
back exactly what the pause recorded: container applications may run their instances
again, each Worker serves the version it served before, at 100%, the queues deliver
again, and the paused Workflow instances carry on. It takes seconds.

Appflare never resumes a paused app by itself. Before you resume, fix what made it run
away, for example by undoing the settings change that started it. If it loops again
and automatic pausing is on, Appflare pauses it again.

**App resumed** (`app_resumed`) goes out when someone other than the person who
paused the app resumes it, and when Appflare puts back a pause that could not finish.

### While an app is paused

- Updates, rollbacks, database restores, settings changes and **Install again** are
  refused with "Resume the app first". **Check now** waits too. A new version would run the app again without anyone resuming it.
- Health checks skip it, so its paused page never counts as an app that is down.
- Uninstalling it works as usual. The uninstall closes the pause.

### If a pause or resume fails

A pause job that fails puts back what it had changed, and the notification channels
get **Pausing an app failed** (`app_pause_failed`): the app may still be running and
using Cloudflare. The job's log says which step failed. Select **Pause app** again
once the cause is fixed, or follow the log. When something cannot be put back, the app
stays paused, and **Resume** finishes putting it back.

Automatic pausing backs off: after a failed automatic pause, Appflare does not try that
app again for an hour.

A resume job that fails leaves the app paused, and Home still offers **Resume**.
Every step of a resume sets a recorded value, so resuming again carries on where the
failed one stopped.

## Pause apps automatically

Automatic pausing is off by default. Appflare only alerts you, and your apps keep
running.

**Settings > Your account > Spend protection** sets what every app follows:

- **Pause apps automatically**, on or off.
- **What pauses an app**: the loop check and, on Workers Paid, a spending cap. Each
  shows which apps it would act on right now.

![Settings, Spend protection: Pause apps automatically on; the loop check at Standard and a $10 monthly cap, each naming Service status; a $50 monthly budget on pace for $66.45; and unusual usage alerts at 20× its normal](/screenshots/settings-spend-protection.png)

On an app's **Usage** tab, **Pause automatically** is **Account default**, **On for
this app** or **Off for this app**. While it is on, the app follows the account's
triggers, or select **Customize for this app** to give it its own. **Use the
account's triggers** goes back.

![An app's Spend protection on its Usage tab: unusual usage at 20× its normal, two usage alerts, and Pause automatically on for this app with its own triggers, paused after passing its $2.00 daily cap](/screenshots/usage-app-spend-protection.png)

When a trigger trips, Appflare pauses the app on that check, the same way
[a pause by hand](#what-a-pause-does) does, and tells you why. You resume it.

### When it loops

A loop is real money and far above the app's normal at once. Both halves must hold, so
a tiny app at 100 times its normal does not trip, and neither does a busy app at its
usual level. The check uses the app's cost over the last 60 minutes at list price,
storage left out. After a resume it counts only what the app used since the resume,
as a rate an hour, so the loop it was paused for does not pause it again at once.

| Level | Workers Paid | Workers Free |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relaxed** | Over $5 an hour and at least 100× its normal | At least 100× its normal and on pace to use at least 50% of a free daily limit within the day |
| **Standard** (default) | Over $1 an hour and at least 50× its normal | At least 50× its normal and on pace to use at least 25% of a free daily limit within the day |
| **Strict** | Over $0.25 an hour and at least 20× its normal | At least 20× its normal and on pace to use at least 10% of a free daily limit within the day |

On Workers Free, R2 activity costing more than the level's dollars an hour also counts
as real money, since R2 bills on Free too.

One more sign counts as far above normal at any level: a single Durable Object that ran
its alarm more than 3,600 times in an hour, more than once a second. Only a loop does
that.

The check must trip on two checks in a row, 5 minutes apart, before Appflare pauses
the app. An app with no normal yet, such as one installed today, is judged by the money
half alone, so a loop right after an install still pauses it.

### When it spends more than a cap

On Workers Paid, a spending cap pauses an app once its activity at list price passes
an amount **today**, **this week** or **this month**. The account's default cap is
$10 a month. The cap counts the whole period, a resume or not. Storage does not count: a pause cannot stop storage charges, so it would
trip the cap again after every resume. The cap's bar shows what is spent, where the
pace leads, and when the cap resets. Periods are UTC, as for
[usage alerts](#usage-alerts).

### Resume until the cap resets

An app that is still past its cap when you resume it would be paused again on the
next check, whatever paused it first. Resume then offers **Resume until the cap
resets**: the cap is set
aside until its period ends, at 00:00 UTC for a daily cap, and the loop check keeps
running. The dialog links to the app's cap if you would rather change it.

## Monthly budget

On Workers Paid, **Settings > Your account > Spend protection > Monthly budget** keeps
the whole account's bill under an amount: "Keep this month's bill under $50 including
the $5 plan". It is off by default, and the amount starts at $50 when you turn it on.

The bill it counts is the $5 plan plus the account's usage past what the plan
includes, projected to the end of the month at the current pace. It is the part a
pause can lower, unlike the list-price spend of usage alerts and caps.

When the projected bill reaches the budget, Appflare tells you once that month
(**Monthly budget reached**, `budget_reached`) and pauses the app with the largest
projected share of the month's usage past included. It pauses one app per check, every
5 minutes, until the projection fits, counting paused apps as stopped. The budget pauses
apps even when their automatic pausing is off. The section shows the bill against the
budget, and which app it would pause first.

An app that costs more to stop than to pay for can opt out: on its **Usage** tab, tick
**Never pause this app for the budget**. Its usage still counts toward the bill, so
Appflare pauses other apps first. When no app Appflare may pause adds to the bill, the
budget pauses nothing more.

## Workers Free and Workers Paid

Appflare uses the account's Workers plan from **Settings > Your account > What this
account can run** ([how it finds the plan](/guides/install-apps/#workers-free-or-workers-paid)).

On **Workers Paid**, Cloudflare bills $5 a month plus usage past what the plan includes.
Dollar alerts, spending caps and the monthly budget all apply.

On **Workers Free**, Cloudflare cannot bill you for Workers: past a daily limit it
refuses instead. So there is no spending cap and no monthly budget. Usage alerts and the
loop check measure shares of the free daily limits, and Appflare warns before a limit
runs out. R2 is the exception: it bills past its free amount on any plan, so Home's **Can
be billed** shows what R2 costs when an app is past it.

## Permissions

Usage needs one Cloudflare permission: **Account Analytics: Read**. How you get it
depends on [how Appflare connects](/guides/cloudflare-connection/):

- **Cloudflare sign-in** asks for it. An Appflare connected before this version lacks
  it, and the **Sign-in permissions** row under **What this account can run** asks an
  admin to [Reconnect Cloudflare](/guides/cloudflare-connection/#reconnect-cloudflare).
- **An API token** lists Account Analytics in the token form under **Usage and spend
  protection**, and the template link selects it. It is optional. See
  [the token's permissions](/start/install/#1-connect-cloudflare).

Without Account Analytics, Appflare reads no usage, so it sends no usage alerts and
pauses nothing on its own. Home's **Usage** section asks you to reconnect Cloudflare
(or, with a token, to give it the permission) instead of showing numbers, the app
cards leave out their usage, each app's **Usage** tab says **Reconnect to see usage**
or **Give the token access to usage**, and **Settings > Your account > Spend
protection** says it has nothing to act on yet. Installing, updating and everything
else work as before.

![Home on a Cloudflare sign-in without the usage permission: a Sign-in permissions row under Needs attention and the Usage section, each with Reconnect Cloudflare](/screenshots/usage-reconnect-home.png)

Once it has the permission, spend protection starts with the first numbers it reads.

## What the numbers are

Every amount is usage times Cloudflare's list price. It is an estimate, not your bill:
Cloudflare rounds some meters up, and **Billing** in the Cloudflare dashboard has the
real amounts. **Cost past included** and the monthly bill split the account's usage
past included across apps by each app's share of each product.

Appflare estimates with these prices and allowances, from Cloudflare's pricing pages:

| Worker requests | 10 million a month | $0.30 per million | 100,000 a day |
| Worker CPU time | 30 million ms a month | $0.02 per million ms | Limited per request, not per day |
| Durable Objects requests | 1 million a month | $0.15 per million | 100,000 a day |
| Durable Objects duration | 400,000 GB-s a month | $12.50 per million GB-s | 13,000 GB-s a day |
| Durable Objects rows read | 25 billion a month | $0.001 per million | 5 million a day |
| Durable Objects rows written | 50 million a month | $1.00 per million | 100,000 a day |
| Durable Objects storage | 5 GB | $0.20 per GB-month | 5 GB |
| D1 rows read | 25 billion a month | $0.001 per million | 5 million a day |
| D1 rows written | 50 million a month | $1.00 per million | 100,000 a day |
| D1 storage | 5 GB | $0.75 per GB-month | 5 GB |
| KV reads | 10 million a month | $0.50 per million | 100,000 a day |
| KV writes, deletes and lists | 1 million a month each | $5.00 per million | 1,000 a day each |

| Container disk | 200 GB-hours a month | $0.00000007 per GB-s | Not available |

Some usage cannot be told apart by app, or is not counted at all:

- **Workers AI** has no per-app numbers in any Cloudflare API. Appflare splits the
  account's Workers AI use evenly across the apps that bind Workers AI.
- **Analytics Engine** is counted by dataset name. Cloudflare publishes a price but
  does not charge it yet, so it costs $0 here.
- **A Workflow's CPU time and runs** count as its Worker's, as Cloudflare bills them.
  Its steps are their own product.
- **Workers Logs** are not counted. Cloudflare bills them per log event today, and from
  December 1, 2026 under its Observability pricing.
- **Anything Appflare did not install**, such as Appflare's own Worker or Workers you
  deployed yourself, is not read. It still uses the account's included usage and free daily limits, so the
  account's real totals can be higher than Appflare's.

For a check that does not depend on Appflare, Cloudflare can email you when the
account's usage-based spend for the billing period passes an amount. In the Cloudflare
dashboard, open **Manage Account > Billing > Billable Usage** and select **Create
budget alert** (see Cloudflare's
[Budget alerts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/billing/manage/budget-alerts/)). It
only tells you; it does not pause anything.
